The Element Road

The Element Road is a third-person RPG that sets in a world possessed with magic. The player's gifted talent grants the power to retrieve elements from surrounding objects, thus create objects or spells to solve daily requests or fight against monsters. The player embarks on their own journey.

Core Mechanics

Grab Element System

Mechanic Overview

The Grab Element System is a core gameplay mechanic that allows players to extract elemental properties from naturally occurring objects in the environment. These elements serve as the foundation for spell crafting and certain item crafting interactions.

Players can hold up to two active elements, one in each hand. By acquiring different elements from their surroundings, players can experiment with elemental combinations to create spells and overcome challenges during exploration, puzzle-solving, and combat.

Element Sources

Each element is associated with specific environmental sources. Players must identify and interact with these sources to acquire the elements required for crafting.

Element Example Sources
Earth Stones, Natural Pathways
Water Ponds, Rivers, Lakes
Wind Wind Chimes, Strong Wind on Bridges
Wood Trees

To maintain consistency within the game's elemental system, only pure, naturally occurring materials can serve as elemental sources. For example, players can extract Wood from a living tree, but not from processed wooden planks or furniture.

Player Interaction

When the player hovers their cursor over a valid elemental source, the cursor changes into a pointing hand to indicate that the object is interactable.

Left-clicking acquires the element into the player's left hand, while right-clicking assigns it to the right hand. The corresponding HUD element slot updates immediately to display the newly acquired element.

Each hand can hold only one active element. Acquiring another element automatically replaces the previously held element, allowing players to adapt their elemental combinations without cooldown restrictions.

Design Rationale

The Grab Element System was designed to make environmental interaction a fundamental part of gameplay rather than a secondary activity. By requiring players to obtain elements from their surroundings, the system encourages exploration, environmental awareness, and strategic decision-making.

Limiting players to one element per hand introduces a deliberate constraint on resource management. Players must consider which elements to retain and when to replace them, particularly when preparing for combat or solving environmental puzzles.

Allowing elements to be replaced instantly prevents the system from becoming overly restrictive, supporting experimentation with different combinations. Together with clear visual feedback, these decisions aim to create an intuitive mechanic that connects exploration, spell crafting, and combat into a cohesive gameplay loop.

Spell Crafting System

Mechanic Overview

The Spell Crafting System is a core gameplay mechanic that allows players to combine elements acquired from the environment to create spells. Rather than unlocking every spell through conventional progression, players are encouraged to experiment with elemental combinations to discover new abilities.

Each spell is associated with a predefined elemental recipe. By combining the elements held in their left and right hands, players can craft spells with different combat and utility functions. These spells can then be used to overcome enemies, interact with the environment, and solve puzzles.

Player Interaction

Spell crafting is directly connected to the Grab Element System. Players must first acquire two elements from environmental sources before attempting to craft a spell.

  1. Acquire elements from the environment using the Grab Element System.
  2. Assign the desired elements to the left and right hands.
  3. Press F to attempt spell crafting.
  4. The system checks whether the selected combination matches a valid spell recipe.
  5. If a valid recipe is found, both elements are consumed and the corresponding spell is crafted.
  6. The crafted spell is assigned to the spell slot, updating its icon and available number of uses.

If no valid recipe exists, the crafting attempt fails without consuming the currently held elements. This allows players to experiment with different combinations without losing resources through unsuccessful attempts.

Element Combinations

The system uses predefined recipes to determine the resulting spell. Each recipe specifies the required elements and the corresponding spell to be created.

Element order can influence the crafting outcome. For example, combining Wood in the left hand with Water in the right hand may produce a different spell from combining Water with Wood.

Left Hand Right Hand Result
Wood Water Spell A
Water Wood Spell B

The combinations above illustrate the order-dependent recipe system. Some recipes may produce the same spell regardless of element order.

Spell Behaviour

Once crafted, spells provide different ways for players to interact with enemies and the environment. Each spell has predefined attributes, including damage, range, size, duration, travel speed, and available uses, depending on its intended behaviour.

Spells are classified into several categories based on their gameplay functions.

Cast Type Gameplay Function
Projectile Launches a projectile towards a target or direction.
Area of Effect (AoE) Applies an effect within a designated area, either instantly or over time.
Crowd Control (CC) Applies negative status effects, such as stun or freeze, to restrict enemy actions.
Spawn Object Creates temporary objects within the game world.

Spell Casting and Targeting

Players can cast their currently equipped spell by pressing R. Before casting, the system validates the spell's remaining uses and targeting requirements.

Different spells use different targeting methods. Some spells can be directed freely using the cursor, while others require a valid ground location or a specific target actor.

Upon successful casting, the spell executes its predefined behaviour and consumes one use. Once all available uses have been depleted, the spell can no longer be cast. Players must craft another spell to replace or replenish their available abilities.

Visual Feedback

The Spell Crafting System communicates the player's current spell through a dedicated HUD slot, displaying the spell icon and remaining uses. Crafting a new spell updates this information immediately.

Individual spells also use distinctive visual effects and impact effects to communicate their behaviour, helping players recognise different abilities during combat and exploration.

Design Rationale

The Spell Crafting System was designed to establish a direct relationship between environmental exploration, player experimentation, and combat progression. Rather than providing players with a fixed collection of abilities, the system encourages them to discover spells by interacting with the game world and experimenting with elemental combinations.

The decision to support order-dependent recipes increases the number of possible outcomes from a limited selection of elements. This introduces additional depth to the crafting system and rewards players who experiment with different arrangements.

Limiting each crafted spell to a predefined number of uses encourages players to consider when and where to use their abilities. Combined with the two-element carrying restriction, this creates meaningful decisions around elemental selection, spell preparation, and resource management.

By supporting multiple spell categories and targeting methods, the system also allows players to approach encounters in different ways. The overall design aims to create a flexible gameplay experience in which experimentation and environmental awareness are essential to player progression.
