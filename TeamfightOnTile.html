TeamFight on Tiles

TeamFight on Tiles is a hex-grid auto-battler inspired by Teamfight Tactics, with an additional focus on terrain-based strategy. While retaining the core concept of positioning units and letting them battle automatically, the game introduces different tile types, each with its own movement cost.

These terrain variations influence unit movement and pathfinding, adding an extra layer of strategy to unit positioning and battlefield navigation. Players must consider not only their team composition but also how the terrain affects combat.

Hex Gird

The game features a 45-tile hexagonal grid arranged across seven rows, with each row containing six to seven tiles. The board size was determined through multiple prototypes to provide sufficient space for unit movement and strategic positioning without making the battlefield feel too sparse. The grid is divided into different zones, with the three rows closest to the player designated for unit placement and the three furthest rows used for enemy spawning. This layout creates a clear separation between opposing teams while leaving space for units to navigate and engage in combat.

Terrain

To introduce additional strategic depth, the grid features four terrain types: Road, Grass, Water, and Mountain, each represented by a different colour. These terrain types influence unit movement and pathfinding, with certain tiles allowing normal movement while others restrict movement entirely. Each terrain type has its own movement properties, affecting how units navigate across the battlefield. Impassable tiles cannot be traversed.

Pathfinding Behaviour

The flowchart below illustrates how units automatically locate, approach, and attack enemies during combat. Each unit searches for a target and checks whether it is within attack range. If the target is out of range, the unit uses the A* pathfinding algorithm to calculate a route towards a neighbouring tile of the target, taking terrain movement costs and obstacles into account.

After moving to the next tile, the unit checks its attack range again and recalculates its path if the target has moved. This process continues until the target is defeated, at which point the unit searches for a new enemy and repeats the cycle.

                            B --> C{Target in Attack Range?}

                            C -->|Yes| D[Attack Target]
                            C -->|No| E[Calculate A* Path]

                            E --> F[Move to Next Tile]
                            F --> G{Target in Attack Range?}

                            G -->|Yes| D
                            G -->|No| H{Target Moved?}

                            H -->|Yes| E
                            H -->|No| I[Continue Along Path]
                            I --> F
                            D --> J{Target Defeated?}
                            J -->|Yes| A
                            J -->|No| C
